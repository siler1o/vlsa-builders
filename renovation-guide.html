Renovating a turnover house in Cavite: what to do first.

A practical order of work for new homeowners, so you only pay for each job once.

Many new homes in Cavite subdivisions are turned over with bare finishes: plain walls, basic floors, few outlets and no built-ins. Upgrading is exciting, but doing things in the wrong order is the most common way renovations go over budget. Tiles get broken to add pipes, ceilings get opened to add lights, and fresh paint gets scratched by cabinet installers.

This guide covers what to check, what to ask first, and the order that keeps each finished surface safe.

1. Check the house before you change it

Before any upgrade, walk through the house with a notebook and your phone camera.

  • Cracks and leaks. Look at ceilings, wall corners, window edges and the roof deck after rain.
  • Doors and windows. Open and close each one. Note anything that sticks or does not lock.
  • Water. Run every faucet and flush. Check pressure and look under sinks for drips.
  • Power. Count the outlets in each room and test them. Note where you will need more.

If your developer still accepts punch-list items, report defects before you renovate over them.

2. Ask permission first

Extensions, carports, facade changes and anything structural usually need approval.

  • Homeowners' association. Most subdivisions have rules on extensions, setbacks, gate designs and exterior colours.
  • Building permit. Ask your municipality's Office of the Building Official which permits your project needs. Extensions and structural work usually need one.

Getting approvals sorted first avoids work being stopped halfway, which costs more than the paperwork.

3. Do the work in this order

Each step protects the one before it. Skipping ahead means redoing finished work.

  1. Structure, roof and waterproofingExtensions, walls, roof repairs and waterproofing of roof decks and wet areas. Water ruins everything that comes after.
  2. Electrical and plumbing rough-insNew outlets, circuits, light points and water lines, while walls and ceilings are still open.
  3. Ceilings and partitionsClose up the ceiling and build dividing walls once the wires and pipes are in.
  4. Tiles and flooringFloors, kitchen and bathroom walls, stair cladding. This is the messiest step, so it comes before paint.
  5. PaintInterior walls and ceilings, then the exterior.
  6. Cabinets, doors and fixturesBuilt-ins, doors, lights, faucets and the finishing touches, measured after the room is done.

4. Plan these before the ceiling goes up

  • Where every outlet and switch goes, including aircon, ref, washer and water heater.
  • Where the lights go: downlights, a feature light, outdoor lights.
  • Where the sink, shower and any water heater will be.
  • Where built-ins will stand, so outlets are not hidden behind a wardrobe.

5. Budget with a written scope

A clear scope is the best protection against surprise costs.

  • Ask for a quote that lists the work room by room, and what is not included.
  • Agree on materials and finishes in writing: tile type, ceiling panel, paint colour, cabinet finish.
  • Set aside 10 to 15 percent of your budget for surprises found once walls or floors are opened.
  • Agree on the payment schedule before work starts.

6. What to send a contractor for a quote

  • Photos of each area, taken from the corners of the room.
  • Rough measurements, or the floor plan from your developer.
  • Your location and subdivision.
  • Photos of finishes you like, and your target timeline.

With these, a contractor can give a first estimate before visiting, and the site check becomes about confirming details.
